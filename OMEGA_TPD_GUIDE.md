# ⌚ Omega Co-Axial Movement TPD & Winding Guide

**Omega** has redefined horological standards with the **Co-Axial Master Chronometer** movements. To maintain the high-frequency stability and METAS-certified precision of these timepieces, consistent and correct winding is essential.

## 🛠 The Co-Axial Advantage
Whether it is the **Calibre 8900** or the legendary **Calibre 3861**, Omega movements are designed for extreme durability. However, maintaining a consistent mainspring tension ensures the constant force required for the Co-Axial escapement to perform at its peak.

## 📊 Omega TPD Data Table (Key Calibers)

| Omega Series | Movement (Calibre) | TPD Setting | Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seamaster 300M** | Cal. 8800 | 650 - 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Planet Ocean / Aqua Terra** | Cal. 8900 / 8901 | 650 - 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Speedmaster (Auto)** | Cal. 9300 / 9900 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Speedmaster Moonwatch** | Cal. 3861 (Manual)* | N/A | Hand Wind Only |
| **Constellation** | Cal. 8700 / 8800 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |

> **💡 Tech Tip:** While modern Omega Master Chronometers are anti-magnetic to 15,000 Gauss, placing them in a high-quality winder prevents the lubricants from congealing during long periods of non-wear.
> 
> *\*Note: For manual wind models like the Speedmaster Professional, we recommend manual winding at the same time each day.*
