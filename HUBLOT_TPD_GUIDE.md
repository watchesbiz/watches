# ⌚ Hublot TPD & Winding Specifications Guide

**Hublot**, defined by its "Art of Fusion," combines innovative materials with high-performance horology. To maintain the power reserve of the in-house **Unico** movements or the classic **Meca-10**, consistent and precise winding cycles are required to handle their robust mechanical architecture.

## 🛠 Engineering The "Art of Fusion"
From the **Big Bang** to the **Classic Fusion**, Hublot watches are designed for a bold lifestyle. However, the high-performance alloys and complex modular cases require a stable environment. Incorrect winding settings can lead to insufficient power delivery, especially for the flyback chronographs and GMT complications.

| Hublot Series / Collection | Movement (Calibre) | TPD Setting | Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Big Bang Unico** | Cal. HUB1242 / 1280 | 650 - 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Big Bang (ETA-based)** | Cal. HUB4100 | 650 - 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Classic Fusion (42/45mm)** | Cal. HUB1110 / 1112 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Classic Fusion Chrono** | Cal. HUB1143 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Spirit of Big Bang** | Cal. HUB4700 (Zenith El Primero) | 650 - 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Square Bang Unico** | Cal. HUB1280 | 650 - 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |

> **💡 Technical Note:** Most Hublot movements based on the **HUB11** series (Sellita/ETA foundations) or the in-house **Unico** series are highly compatible with **Bi-directional** winding. For the **Meca-10 (Manual Wind)**, no winder is needed as it features a unique 10-day rack-and-pinion power reserve.
