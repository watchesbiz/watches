# ⌚ Audemars Piguet (AP) TPD & Winding Specifications Guide

**Audemars Piguet** timepieces represent the pinnacle of *Haute Horlogerie*. Whether you own a vintage **Royal Oak "Jumbo"** or a modern **Code 11.59**, maintaining the mainspring's optimal tension is critical for the longevity of these hand-finished calibers.

## 🛠 The AP Engineering Standard
Audemars Piguet movements are known for their sophisticated oscillating weights and ultra-thin profiles. Using an unshielded or incorrectly programmed winder can lead to "slipping" of the winding click or accuracy deviations due to magnetic interference.

## 📊 Audemars Piguet TPD Data Table (Core Models)

| AP Series / Collection | Movement (Calibre) | TPD Setting | Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Royal Oak "Jumbo"** | Cal. 2121 / 7121 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Royal Oak Selfwinding (41mm)** | Cal. 3120 / 4302 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Royal Oak Offshore (Chrono)** | Cal. 3126 / 4401 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Royal Oak Concept** | Cal. 2965 / 4401 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Code 11.59 Selfwinding** | Cal. 4302 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Millenary 4101** | Cal. 4101 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |

> **💡 Technical Insight:** While most modern AP calibers like the **4302** are bi-directional, older ultra-thin calibers perform best at a stable **650 TPD** to prevent excessive wear on the winding bridge.
