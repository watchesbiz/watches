# ⌚ Cartier Watch TPD & Winding Specifications Guide

**Cartier**, the "Jeweler of Kings and King of Jewelers," has a rich horological history. From the pioneering **Santos** to the sophisticated **Drive de Cartier**, maintaining the precise tension of their in-house MC calibers is essential for preserving both their aesthetic and functional value.

## 🛠 The 1847 MC & 1904 MC Standards
Modern Cartier timepieces often utilize the robust **1847 MC** and **1904 MC** in-house movements. These movements are engineered for daily wear, but like all precision instruments, they perform best when kept at a stable power reserve through consistent, bi-directional winding.

## 📊 Cartier TPD Data Table (Key Collections)

| Cartier Series / Collection | Movement (Calibre) | TPD Setting | Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ballon Bleu (42mm)** | Cal. 1847 MC | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Santos de Cartier** | Cal. 1847 MC | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Tank MC** | Cal. 1904-PS MC | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Pasha de Cartier** | Cal. 1847 MC | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Drive de Cartier** | Cal. 1904-FU MC | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Calibre de Cartier** | Cal. 1904 MC | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |

> **💡 Technical Insight:** Most modern Cartier self-winding calibers are optimized for **Bi-directional** winding. For smaller Ladies' models with ETA-based movements, a lower TPD of **650** is generally recommended to avoid over-stressing the mainspring.
