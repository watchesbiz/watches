# ⌚ IWC Schaffhausen TPD & Winding Specifications Guide

**IWC Schaffhausen** is renowned for its engineering-led approach to horology. From the robust **Pilot’s Watches** to the elegant **Portugieser**, maintaining the optimal power reserve of an IWC movement—especially those equipped with the legendary **Pellaton Winding System**—requires precise TPD settings.

## 🛠 Engineering Highlight: The Pellaton System
Many IWC in-house movements, such as the **52000-calibre family**, utilize the Pellaton automatic winding system. This pawl-winding design is exceptionally efficient but benefits from consistent, bi-directional tension to maintain its impressive 7-day power reserve without unnecessary mechanical stress.

## 📊 IWC TPD Data Table (Key Collections)

| IWC Series / Collection | Movement (Calibre) | TPD Setting | Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Big Pilot’s Watch** | Cal. 52110 / 52111 | 800 - 950 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Portugieser Chronograph** | Cal. 69355 | 600 - 800 | Clockwise (CW) |
| **Portugieser Automatic 7-Day** | Cal. 52010 | 800 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Pilot’s Watch Mark XVIII / XX** | Cal. 35111 / 32111 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Portofino Automatic** | Cal. 35111 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |
| **Ingenieur Automatic 40** | Cal. 32111 | 650 | Both (Bi-directional) |

> **💡 Technical Note:** While the 50000 series (7-day reserve) is highly efficient with bi-directional winding, many IWC chronographs based on the 69000 or 79000 series calibers are **Uni-directional (Clockwise)**. Always verify your specific caliber.
