# ⌚ Patek Philippe Movement TPD & Winding Direction Guide

Maintaining a **Patek Philippe** requires a deep understanding of its horological soul. Unlocking its potential requires precision. Unlike many standard movements, Patek's calibers—especially the legendary ultra-thin **Calibre 240** and the **Calibre 324/26-330**—have specific winding requirements to maintain their exquisite complications.

## 🛠 The Precision Challenge
Patek Philippe watches often feature intricate complications (Perpetual Calendars, Chronographs, and Minute Repeaters). Using an incorrect winding direction or an unshielded winder with magnetic interference can disrupt the delicate balance of these masterworks, leading to unexpected movement amplitude drops or timekeeping deviation.

## 📊 Patek Philippe TPD Data Table (Core Calibers)

| Patek Philippe Series | Movement (Calibre) | TPD Setting | Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nautilus / Aquanaut** | Cal. 324 S C / 26-330 | 650 - 800 | Clockwise (CW) |
| **Calatrava (Micro-rotor)** | Cal. 240 | 650 - 800 | Counter-Clockwise (CCW) |
| **Grand Complications** | Cal. R 27 / CH 29 | 800 | Both / CW (Varies) |
| **Annual Calendar** | Cal. 324 S QA | 800 | Clockwise (CW) |
| **Twenty~4 Automatic** | Cal. 324 S C | 650 | Clockwise (CW) |

> **⚠️ Critical Note:** Unlike Rolex, many Patek Philippe calibers are **Uni-directional** (Single direction). Setting your automatic watch winder to "Both" may result in the mainspring not being wound at all, eventually draining the power reserve.
